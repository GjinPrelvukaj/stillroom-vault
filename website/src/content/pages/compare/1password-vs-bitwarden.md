## Side by side

| What | 1Password | Bitwarden |
|---|---|---|
| Free plan | No; 14-day trial | Yes, unlimited passwords and devices |
| One person | $3.99 a month, billed yearly | $19.80 a year for Premium |
| Family | 5 people, $5.99 a month, billed yearly | 6 people, $47.88 a year |
| Encryption | End-to-end, AES-GCM-256; account password plus a 128-bit Secret Key | End-to-end, AES-256 with HMAC; PBKDF2 or Argon2id |
| Source | Proprietary | Open source |
| Self-hosting | No | Yes |
| Two-factor codes | Included | Premium and Families |
| File storage | 1 GB per person | 5 GB on Premium |
| Platforms | macOS, Windows, Linux, iOS, Android, browser extensions | macOS, Windows, Linux, iOS, Android, web, browser extensions, CLI |

Prices are from each company's pricing page in October 2026. 1Password often discounts the first year for new customers.

## Where 1Password is better

- **Polish.** 1Password puts a lot into its apps and autofill, and it shows. That matters most for the people in a family who just want it to work.
- **A Secret Key.** Your vault is locked by your account password and a 128-bit Secret Key that never leaves your devices, so a leaked copy of your vault can't be cracked by guessing your password alone.
- **Travel Mode.** You can remove chosen vaults from your devices before crossing a border, and bring them back later.
- **Two-factor codes on every plan.** Bitwarden keeps its authenticator for paid plans.

## Where Bitwarden is better

- **Price.** The free plan is enough for many people, and Premium costs a fraction of 1Password.
- **Open source.** Anyone can read the code, and it has outside security reviews every year.
- **Your own server.** You can self-host Bitwarden, so your vault syncs through a machine you control.
- **A bigger family plan.** Six people instead of five, for less.

## What both have in common

Both keep a copy of your vault in the cloud so every device stays in step, both encrypt it on your device first, and both fill passwords in your browser and on your phone. That sync is the point of a password manager, and it is the right trade for everyday logins.

It also means both hold everything in one account. If you keep your two-factor codes in the same password manager as your passwords, one place holds every factor. Many people keep their master password, recovery codes and two-factor backup codes somewhere separate; [storing two-factor backup codes](/guides/store-two-factor-backup-codes/) covers the options.

## Where Stillroom Vault fits

Stillroom Vault isn't a password manager: it has no autofill, no phone app and no sync. It is an encrypted vault for one Mac that never goes online, and it sits well next to either one:

- **The keys to your password manager.** Your 1Password Emergency Kit or Bitwarden recovery code, kept off the cloud they protect.
- **Files.** ID scans, contracts, tax records, photos and recordings of any size, opened in built-in viewers without writing a decrypted copy to disk.
- **Pay once.** Free for 25 items, $39 once for one person's Macs, or $129 once for a family of five.
