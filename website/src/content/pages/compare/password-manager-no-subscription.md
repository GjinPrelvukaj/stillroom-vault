## Why people look for a password manager without a subscription

A password manager is something you keep for years, so a small monthly fee adds up. Some people would also rather not depend on a company's billing system for access to their own passwords: if a card expires or a plan changes, they want to know their vault still opens.

There's a fair point on the other side. A password manager that syncs through its own servers has running costs every month, and a subscription pays for them. Apps that keep your vault on your own devices don't have those costs, which is why most of the one-time and free options below are offline or local-first apps. [The best offline password managers for Mac](/compare/offline-password-manager-mac/) goes into that side of things.

All prices below are in US dollars and were checked on 30 October 2026. They change, so check before you buy.

## Free, with no subscription at all

**KeePassXC** is free and open source under the GPLv3, for macOS, Windows and Linux. Its site promises no cloud, no ads and no subscriptions. There's no item limit. It keeps your passwords in a `.kdbx` file on your Mac, fills logins in your browser through its extension, and shows two-factor codes. See [Stillroom Vault vs KeePassXC](/compare/keepassxc/).

**MacPass** is a free, open source (GPLv3) KeePass app made only for macOS. It opens the same `.kdbx` files as KeePassXC, with Auto-Type and two-factor codes. Download it only from its [GitHub releases page](https://github.com/MacPass/MacPass/releases): its developer says the old web address lapsed.

**Apple Passwords** is free and built into macOS. It fills passwords in Safari and other apps and syncs through iCloud Keychain to your other Apple devices. For many people's everyday logins, it's enough.

There are also free tiers of paid products. **Bitwarden**'s free plan has no limit on passwords or devices, and its paid plans are yearly subscriptions. **Strongbox** has a free version for non-commercial use with fewer convenience features. **Enpass** has a free plan for up to 25 items.

## Paid once: genuine one-time purchases on the Mac

**pwSafe** is $14.99 once on the Mac App Store, from Huvisoft B.V., and its listing describes it as a one-time purchase. It uses the Password Safe format, syncs with its iPhone and iPad app through iCloud or through Dropbox, and fills in Safari. It needs macOS 14 or later.

**Strongbox Pro** offers a lifetime licence for $124.99, alongside $2.99 a month or $24.99 a year. Strongbox is a KeePass app for iPhone, iPad and Mac, open source under the AGPL-3.0, with autofill in browsers and sync through storage you choose. At today's prices the lifetime licence costs about the same as five years of the yearly plan.

**Stillroom Vault** is $39 once for all of one person's Macs, or $79 once for a Family licence for up to 5 people, plus tax where applicable. It's free for 25 items, with every feature. It's our app, so the next section covers it in more detail, limits included.

## Lifetime deals, and what can go wrong

"Lifetime" usually means the lifetime of the product, on the vendor's terms. That's often a good deal, but it pays to read what it covers.

- **Does it include new major versions?** Some lifetime licences cover one version. When version 2 arrives, you either pay again or stay on the old one.
- **Does it depend on a server?** If the app needs the vendor's sync service or sign-in to work, a lifetime licence is only as durable as that service.
- **What if the plan is withdrawn?** Vendors drop lifetime options for new buyers. Existing licences are usually honoured, but the app can stop getting new features. Enpass's pricing page, for example, lists Free, Premium and Family plans and no lifetime option.
- **What if the company changes hands?** Password managers do get bought. Strongbox's site, for example, says it has joined Applause. A change of owner can mean a change of pricing.
- **Third-party deal sites.** If a "lifetime" bundle is sold through another store, check exactly which plan you're getting and that the vendor honours it. Buying from the vendor or the App Store is simplest.

The lowest-risk combination is an app that works offline and keeps your passwords in a format other apps can read. If the company disappears, the app keeps opening your file, and something else can open it too. KeePass files (`.kdbx`) are the best example, with many apps that read them.

## Which password managers went subscription-only

The best-known example is **1Password**. It used to sell standalone licences for 1Password 7, with vaults kept locally. In its community forum, 1Password staff say it no longer sells new licences and is focusing on memberships, and that moving to 1Password 8 needs a membership. A licence you already have keeps working with 1Password 7 for as long as that version runs on macOS.

In September 2026, 1Password announced that 1Password 7 is being retired. From May 2027 it will stop syncing changes for membership accounts. Standalone vaults stay accessible in 1Password 7, which is no longer actively developed, and 1Password recommends moving them to an account so you keep getting critical updates.

1Password today is a subscription: Individual is $3.99 a month, or $2.99 a month billed yearly during a current promotion, and Families is $5.99 a month, or $4.49 billed yearly, for up to 5 people. There's a 14-day free trial and no free plan. It's a capable product with autofill and sync everywhere; [Stillroom Vault vs 1Password](/compare/1password/) covers where it's the better choice, and [moving from 1Password](/moving-from/1password/) covers exporting.

**Bitwarden** has no one-time option, but its free plan is generous. Premium is $19.80 a year, and Families is $47.88 a year for up to 6 people. See [Stillroom Vault vs Bitwarden](/compare/bitwarden/).

## How Stillroom Vault's one-time price works

Stillroom Vault is an encrypted vault for Apple silicon Macs with macOS 26 or later. It holds passwords, live two-factor codes and notes, but it's built around files: scans, contracts, photos, recordings and documents of any size, with folders, tags and earlier versions. It has no account, no server and no sync, and makes no network connections. That's what makes a one-time price sensible: there's no service to keep paying for.

- **Free for 25 items**, with every feature, forever. The limit never stops you opening, exporting or backing up what you have.
- **$39 once** for unlimited items on all of one person's Macs, with every 1.x update included.
- **$79 once** for a Family licence for up to 5 people, each with their own vault on all their Macs.
- **If there's a 2.0**, upgrading will cost $19 for 1.x owners, and 1.x keeps working forever. Nothing renews.
- **The format is public.** A short MIT-licensed script opens a vault without the app, so your data never depends on us. See [the file format](/format/).

Be clear about what you don't get. Stillroom doesn't fill passwords in your browser: you copy and paste, and the clipboard clears after 30 seconds if nothing else was copied since. There's no phone app and no sync. Its source is published under the Functional Source License, which is source available, not open source, and it hasn't had an independent security audit yet; one is planned. If everyday autofill on every device matters most, a free or one-time app from the table above, or a subscription manager, may suit you better.

Many people use Stillroom alongside a password manager: everyday logins where autofill helps, and the things that must never leave the Mac (ID scans, [two-factor backup codes](/guides/store-two-factor-backup-codes/), a password manager's own master password) in Stillroom. Full prices are on the [pricing page](/pricing/).

## How to choose

- **You want to pay nothing:** KeePassXC or MacPass, or Apple Passwords if you're happy with iCloud.
- **You want to pay once, with a phone app:** Strongbox Pro's lifetime licence, or pwSafe.
- **You want to pay once, and keep files as well as passwords offline:** Stillroom Vault.
- **You want autofill and sync on every device and don't mind a subscription:** Bitwarden's free plan or Premium, or 1Password.
