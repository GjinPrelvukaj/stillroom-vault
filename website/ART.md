# Art for stillroomvault.com

Every picture on the site is a slot named after where it sits, filled by `public/art/<slot>.webp`: the site finds the
file by name at build time, so adding or swapping one is a file and a rebuild. Every slot is filled, all in the collage series below. The ten paper washes stay as they are. Without
its file a slot falls back quietly: Learn more, the menu and the features page show a drawn dot ring, and everywhere
else the picture is simply left out.

Add images with `scripts/add-art.py` (below), never by copying them in: it sizes them and sets their paper.

## The look

One series, so every image belongs with the others (the paper washes below are the page's paper, not pictures):

- **Collages with the feel of a museum archive**: a finely detailed vintage watercolour and ink illustration, like a
  plate from a 19th-century naturalist's or architect's book, combined with a soft, grainy, sun-faded photograph.
  Muted, warm palette (aged-paper ochre, faded sky blue, sage, umber, cream), visible grain and fine print noise.
- **A window into a private place**: each picture's subject sits in a rectangular inset panel with a thin
  off-white border, painted in the watercolour; around and behind it the same world continues in the photograph,
  parts of it crossing the border. The frame is always filled, never a lone object on blank paper.
- **Subjects**: the ordinary private things people keep: keys, passports, envelopes and letters, a notebook, a film
  reel, photographs face down, a box with a lid, a desk drawer, among herbs, stone and old wood.
- **Violet** (#6043CA) is the strongest colour in every picture, on the one thing that is "sealed" or important.
- **Never**: text or letters, logos, faces, screens or phones, padlock clip-art, glowing light, neon, 3D renders,
  "hacker" imagery.
- Each prompt below says where its picture sits (beside a heading, a framed card, the home picture) and
  composes for it.

Add to every prompt: *"Style: a mixed-media collage with the feel of a museum archive. A finely detailed vintage watercolour and ink illustration, like a plate from a 19th-century naturalist's or architect's book, combined with a soft, grainy, sun-faded photograph. Muted, warm palette: aged-paper ochre, faded sky blue, sage green, umber and cream, with the one violet (#6043CA) element as the strongest colour in the picture. Visible paper grain and fine print noise over everything, nothing glossy or digital. No text, letters or numbers, no logos, no people's faces, no screens, phones or laptops."*

The prints are full scenes, so they're added as plates (`--plate`, kept as printed) and shown framed with rounded
corners. Export as WebP (quality about 80, under 300 KB) at the size given.

## Per page

No page opens with a full-width banner: pictures are always framed. Pages have their own prints in a second, richer
series: full-colour collages of a vintage naturalist's watercolour and a grainy, sun-faded photograph, with a framed
panel showing the page's subject as a window into a private place. Violet stays the strongest colour. 1536 × 1024,
added with `--plate` so they keep their own colour (without it, add-art.py lifts the paper and washes a full scene out).

- Section hubs (`page-guides`, `page-compare`, `page-moving-from`, `page-learn`, `page-for`): beside the heading.
  Each hub then lays its articles out its own way (`HubCards`).
- Every article, `article-<section>-<slug>` (for example `article-guides-encrypt-files-on-mac`), is shown framed
  under the heading and lead. Until its image exists the article shows its section's print there.
- Security, format, changelog and the legal pages have no picture: they open with a plain heading. Their earlier
  prints (`page-security`, `page-format`, `page-changelog`, `page-legal-*`) aren't shown on the page, but still make
  its link preview.
- Link previews (`scripts/make-og.mjs`) are each page's picture full-bleed, with the wordmark and heading over a fade.
  A page that shows no picture uses its print from the script's `prints` table; add a new page there if it has one.

## Article pieces

Articles share one layout, so what makes each feel like itself is its picture and a few optional pieces, set in its
frontmatter (src/content.config.ts):

- `points`: an "In short" box of three or so lines under the picture (`**bold**` works).
- `quote`: one line from the article itself, set large before its second chapter.
- `picture: false`: no picture, for a short page that reads better plain.
- `theme: 'night'`: the whole page on the dark surface (`.ink`), with no paper washes. Use rarely.
- `demo: 'seal'`: the home page's seal demo after the article.

Every table in an article is set in a tinted panel a little wider than the text, with nothing to set.

## Paper washes

A second, quieter series: not pictures but the paper itself, tone on tone, a few percent darker than the page at
most, with a faint trace of violet. They sit behind text and are meant to be noticed only when you look for them.
Kept few on purpose:

| File | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `wash-ink`, `wash-marble`, `wash-contours`, `wash-folds` | Large washes: ink, marbling, contour lines, old folds | One per article or document (`DocLayout`), behind the heading and opening text |
| `side-ink`, `side-marble`, `side-leaf` | Medium pieces, heavy along their left edge | One or two per long page, lower down, coming in from the screen's edge (mirrored on the right), from 1100px |
| `wash-shadow` | The shadow of lavender and herbs | Behind the closing call to action (`DownloadCta`) |
| `pattern-laid` | Laid writing paper, tiling | The grain of the features page's cards |
| `pattern-herbs` | Scattered herb sprigs, tiling | Behind the download page's heading only (`.herb-band`) |

Each page picks its wash and side pieces, and their height, size, mirroring and turn, from a random seeded by its
address: the same on every build, different on every page, never a pattern. Their strength is one `opacity` per
kind in CSS (`DocLayout`, `DownloadCta`, `features.astro`, `.herb-band`); every edge fades out through a mask.

Add them with `--wash`, which turns the paper clear and keeps only the marks, as ink exactly as it would print on
the site's paper, so they lie over anything with no box:

```bash
python3 scripts/add-art.py --wash ~/Downloads/wash.png wash-ink
```

Generate the large ones at 1536 × 1024, the side pieces at 1024 × 1536 and the patterns at 1024 × 1024 (seamless).
Their prompts are below, under their file names.

## Making them

Generated images go in with `scripts/add-art.py`, which sizes them and lifts their paper to white so they melt into
the page (the site multiplies them onto its own paper). Name each image's slot after it:

```bash
python3 scripts/add-art.py ~/Downloads/keys.png learn-compare ~/Downloads/seal.png page-security
```

Framed slots keep their printed paper and grain instead, shown as a rounded plate: add those with `--plate` first.
They are `home-plate`, `learn-*`, `card-*`, `tier-*` and `menu-*`. Every other slot sits loose beside text and
melts into the page.

Or, with an OpenAI API key, `scripts/make-art.mjs` makes every slot that has no image yet from the prompts below
(`OPENAI_API_KEY=… node scripts/make-art.mjs`, or name slots to remake them).

A `page-` slot replaces the faint ring beside that page's heading as soon as its image exists. After adding any, redraw
the link previews, which show each page's picture:

```bash
npm run build && node scripts/make-og.mjs && npm run build
```
